# Page Intention Alignment — every page as a kept promise

A page is a promise made before it is a layout. This map states, for each
page of the storefront, the **intention we hold with the user** (what the
page is *for* them), the **belief it must strengthen**, the emotional
register it speaks in, what it **gives versus what it asks**, and an honest
audit of where the page could **betray its own intention**. Companion to the
component map, the emotional architecture, and the citations ledger.

The master intention, restated per page so no page forgets it:
**we are on the user's side — the sale is a consequence, not the goal.**

---

## 1. Home (the numbered story 01→05)

**Intention**: *"Meet us. In ten seconds, know who we are; in one scroll,
know why we exist."* The home page is the brand's open door — it must feel
like walking into a clean, calm mill courtyard, not a bazaar.

**Belief strengthened**: real → for people like me.
**Register**: warm certainty, unhurried. Light moves slowly here on purpose.

| Section | Sub-intention | Gives | Asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | "Know us at a glance" | identity, one action, three proof chips, real footage | one click, optionally |
| 01 Absolute Reserve | "See the shelf — it is small on purpose" | curation (Iyengar-Lepper), honest prices | a look, a filter, maybe a click |
| Ticker | "Let the facts settle in" | fluency through repetition | nothing |
| 02 From Native Seeds | "Trust how it is made" | demonstrated process, real numbers | attention, briefly |
| Creed | "Know why we exist" | belief, belonging | nothing |
| 03 Strict Transparency | "Judge us against what you use" | the comparison, the anchor | an honest look |
| 04 Seamless Supply | "Keep the kitchen stocked, save honestly" | real 10%, honest roadmap | a toggle, optionally |
| 05 Testimonials | "Others feed their family with this too" | real voices (real data only) | nothing |

**Betrayal risks**: auto-playing anything; a carousel that moves uninvited;
a headline that shouts. All banned by design. The home page must never
behave like it is in a hurry — the product (slow oil) and the pace of the
page must agree, or the promise breaks in the first ten seconds.

## 2. PDP — the decision room

**Intention**: *"Everything you need to say yes — and nothing that pressures
you."* The PDP is where care (E1) meets proof. It must feel like a
knowledgeable neighbor laying the bottle, the label, and the batch record
on the table — then stepping back.

**Belief strengthened**: safe → worth the price.
**Register**: transparent abundance — more proof than they asked for.

**Gives**: the bottle from every angle (lightbox), three honest price tiers
with per-day cost, the current batch's genealogy (pressed when, what it
yielded), label-matched facts, nutrition, real buyer reviews (4.0–4.7
credible band), the comparison table, FAQs from real WhatsApp questions,
subscription savings computed honestly.
**Asks**: a variant choice, a quantity, one click.

**Betrayal risks**: fake urgency (banned), hidden costs (banned — GST is on
the card), reviews that look manufactured (banned — null-by-design
contract), a sticky bar that covers content (it waits for the CTA to leave
the screen first).

---

## 3. Cart — the holding room

**Intention**: *"Look again, freely. Change your mind; we keep it safe."*
The cart is a pause, not a push. Doubt is welcome here — Festinger says
doubt after choice is normal, and a cart that lets doubt breathe converts
it instead of punishing it.

**Belief strengthened**: easy to act.
**Register**: calm review.

**Gives**: full line review, savings made visible (subscription discount in
rupees), a designed empty state that turns "nothing here" into "reserve
yours — the batch is finite."
**Asks**: nothing until the user moves.

**Betrayal risks**: countdown timers in the cart (a dark pattern — banned);
surprise fees at the next step (Baymard's #1 killer — every cost is visible
before checkout).

---

## 4. Checkout — the handshake

**Intention**: *"Finish quietly. No forms you don't need, no surprises, no
account wall."* Checkout is where the brand's respect becomes operational:
the fewest possible fields, prefill for returning guardians, the full total
before the last click.

**Belief strengthened**: easy to act.
**Register**: quiet competence.

**Gives**: guest checkout always, magic-link account benefit without
password cost, address prefill, GST/discount breakdown, UPI-ready flow.
**Asks**: only what delivery requires.

**Betrayal risks**: every extra field, every forced signup, every
redirect. Baymard's numbers (48% extra-cost abandonment, ~24% forced-account
abandonment) are the audit checklist — each has a designed counter on this
page.

---

## 5. Order confirmation — the remembered peak

**Intention**: *"Rest now. It is done, it is recorded, and we will keep you
posted."* Per the peak-end rule, this page disproportionately becomes "what
buying from Feldnstein feels like." It must end the experience the way the
brand wants to be remembered: calm, specific, human.

**Belief strengthened**: worth returning to.
**Register**: warm closure.

**Gives**: order code, batch reference, savings shown, next steps (pressed
→ packed → shipped), the tracking path, WhatsApp updates.
**Asks**: nothing.

**Betrayal risks**: a bare "order placed" screen (a wasted peak); missing
next-step clarity (manufactures anxiety the brand then pays for in support
tickets).

---

## 6. Track order — anxiety, answered by the user themselves

**Intention**: *"Never wonder where your oil is."* Self-service tracking
respects the guardian's time more than a support queue does.

**Belief strengthened**: safe (post-purchase).
**Gives**: status by code + email, no account needed.
**Betrayal risks**: a tracking page that knows less than the WhatsApp
message would — the two channels must never contradict.

---

## 7. Account — the relationship, remembered

**Intention**: *"Come back to a kitchen that remembers you."* The account
is the brand's side of the relationship: history, one-tap reorder, loyalty
as an unfinished goal (goal-gradient), address ready.

**Belief strengthened**: for people like me → worth returning to.
**Gives**: orders, reorder in one tap, loyalty balance, address book.
**Asks**: a sign-in (magic link — no password, ever).

**Betrayal risks**: an account page that only takes (data) and never gives
(convenience). Every field stored must repay the guardian as saved time.

---

## 8. Policy pages — the written handshake

**Intention**: *"If anything goes wrong, here is exactly what happens."*
Each policy transfers the fear from the buyer to the seller — risk reversal
in writing. The FAQ answers the questions real buyers asked on WhatsApp.

**Belief strengthened**: safe.
**Register**: plain language, signed by a real company (address, FSSAI,
phone).

**Betrayal risks**: legalese that hides; policies that exist but are never
linked from the moment of anxiety (cart, checkout). Linkage is part of the
intention, not decoration.

---

## 9. Sign-in (modal) — identity without cost

**Intention**: *"Your history, one link away — no password to forget, ever."*
**Belief strengthened**: easy to act.
**Betrayal risks**: any friction that makes the account feel like a toll
booth instead of a favor.

---

## Alignment audit — the three laws every page obeys

1. **The page's pace must match the product's pace.** Slow oil, calm pages.
   Any component that hurries the user (autoplay, timers, shouting) breaks
   the brand's own story.
2. **Give before asking, on every page, without exception.** Each page's
   "gives" column must outweigh its "asks" — this is the alignment test a
   redesign must pass.
3. **Real data only, real promises only.** Batch numbers, reviews, stock,
   savings — every claim traceable. One manufactured fact would cost more
   trust than every proof on the site can rebuild (Rozin: contamination is
   sticky; once doubt attaches, it stays).
