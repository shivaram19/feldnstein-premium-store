# Emotional Value Architecture — why a visitor buys cold-pressed oil

The chain the user described, formalized:

**Eye perceives → System 1 feels → meaning attaches → attitude forms → hand buys.**

Zajonc (1980, "Feeling and Thinking: Preferences Need No Inferences") showed
affective judgment precedes and is independent of cognitive evaluation — the
visitor *feels* the page in milliseconds (Lindgaard et al. 2006: ~50 ms) and
rationalizes afterward (Haidt's rider-and-elephant: cognition is the press
secretary of emotion). For a low-involvement staple purchase (Elaboration
Likelihood Model, Petty & Cacioppo 1986), the peripheral route dominates:
imagery, warmth, and trust cues decide; nutrition tables only persuade the
already-health-involved segment.

So the design question is not "what do we say" but "what does the eye make
the heart feel, before a word is read."

---

## The buyer's actual identity

The primary buyer of household cooking oil in this market is the **family
food-guardian** — the person whose self-worth is partly defined by what the
family eats. Haidt's Moral Foundations (care/harm, sanctity/degradation) map
directly onto food: feeding family well is *care*; adulterated oil is
*degradation*. Purchase = moral self-confirmation ("I am a good provider").
Aquino & Reed (2002) on moral identity: people act to protect a self-image
of being caring — the product that lets them *see themselves as protectors*
wins.

## The eight emotional triggers, ranked by purchase power

### E1. Protective Care — *"what I feed them is who I am"* (primary motivator)
The deepest driver. Not health anxiety — love, expressed through the kitchen.
Research: care/harm is the most universal moral foundation (Haidt & Graham
2007, across cultures); moral-identity priming increases pro-social and
protective purchase behavior (Aquino & Reed 2002).
- **Eye triggers:** family table, hands pouring, children eating; copy written
  to the guardian ("for your family's health and wellbeing" — the feldnstein.com
  footer line is exactly this register).
- **Existing components:** testimonial voices, product-story tone.
- **Strengthen:** guardian-perspective imagery above the fold.

### E2. Purity Relief — *"finally, oil I don't have to doubt"* (conviction creator)
The Indian edible-oil category carries real adulteration history (argemone
tragedies, solvent-extraction norms) — buyers live in low-grade vigilance.
Rozin, Millman & Nemeroff (1986, UPenn), the "laws of contagion":
contamination is the most sticky food emotion; once doubt attaches it is
near-impossible to remove ("once in contact, always in contact"). The brand's job: end vigilance. The felt emotion is *relief* —
anxiety switched off.
- **Eye triggers:** "Lab Verified · Batch 042", FSSAI seal, sealed amber glass,
  the batch number as a first-class visual citizen, QC per batch.
- **Existing components:** footer badges, process cards, facts panel, quality
  checks in the admin story (batch traceability).
- **Strengthen:** per-batch QC proof on the PDP (a batch number the buyer can
  verify) — turns the claim into evidence.

### E3. Nostalgia — *"the taste of my grandmother's kitchen"* (access emotion)
The door-opener. Wildschut et al. (2006, Univ. of Southampton, *Emotion*):
nostalgia increases social connectedness, meaning, and self-continuity —
and is triggered by sensory cues (smell/taste memory) and *objects from the
past* (the stone churn, the bull). Nostalgic framing measurably increases
willingness to pay for consumer products.
- **Eye triggers:** the Gaanuga itself — stone, bull, 3 RPM; words like
  "the ancient way", "your grandmother knew this oil"; warm earth tones.
- **Existing components:** process section, creed, brand copy.
- **Strengthen:** first-person sensory copy ("the aroma when the stone starts
  turning") rather than third-person description.

### E4. Authenticity — *"the real thing, proven at the source"* (rationalizer)
Authenticity converts emotion into justified conviction. Kolar & Žabkar
(2014, *Annals of Tourism Research*) consumer-authenticity model: authenticity
is inferred from *indexical cues* — physical traces of origin (the actual
mill, the actual farmer) — more than from claims. Newman & Dhar: authentic
positioning commands price premiums and trust.
- **Eye triggers:** real location (Mothadaka), real press, farmer directory,
  batch → press-session genealogy.
- **Existing components:** process transparency, footer mill address,
  traceability data model.
- **Strengthen:** the farm-tour / mill imagery was the strongest indexical
  cue (removed on request — if it returns, frame it as authenticity proof).

### E5. Tradition Continuity — *"my mother's method, kept alive"* (identity)
Self-continuity across generations (Sedikides, Wildschut et al. 2008):
people buy products that link them to who they've been and where they come
from. The bull-driven press is not retro-styling; it is *unbroken practice* —
3 RPM is credibility because slowness is the proof of method.
- **Eye triggers:** "3 RPM · stone churn" numbers, the word Gaanuga explained
  once and then owned.

### E6. Ritual Sanctity — *"oil for the lamp, not just the pan"* (occasion depth)
Sesame (til) oil in this culture carries religious weight — Saturday lamps,
ritual use. Purchases with sacred framing are the most loyalty-locked
(sanctity foundation; switching feels like transgression). A line like
"pure enough for the lamp" on the sesame PDP unlocks a second buying mission.
- **Existing components:** "Vedic Black Sesame" naming already whispers this.
- **Strengthen:** one honest line on ritual purity — never exploitative.

### E7. Earned Premium — *"I don't compromise on what my family eats"* (status of conscience)
Conspicuous *conscientiousness*: the premium price is the point — it signals
the buyer's priorities to themselves and their circle (similar mechanism to
"conspicuous conservation," Griskevicius et al. 2010). The price must never
apologize; it must *certify*.
- **Eye triggers:** confident price typography, no discount-clutter, the word
  "Reserve".

### E8. Farmer Reciprocity — *"the farmer is paid, and it shows in the seed"* (warm glow)
Farm-direct traceability produces the "warm glow" of fair trade (Andreoni
1989). Secondary, but it differentiates from industrial brands with zero
provenance.
- **Existing components:** farm-sourced process card, supplier story in the
  platform backend.

## The single sentence

The visitor buys when the page makes them feel: **"This is the oil my
grandmother would recognize, proven pure without me having to check, and
feeding my family with it makes me the provider I want to be."**

Nostalgia opens the door (E3) → purity relief removes the doubt (E2) →
care closes the sale (E1) → authenticity, tradition, and earned premium
justify the price after the fact (E4, E5, E7) → ritual and reciprocity
deepen lock-in (E6, E8).

## Design laws that follow

1. **Emotion is carried by images, not adjectives.** The bull, the stone,
   the golden pour, hands, the shed — these fire before copy is read
   (affective primacy). Adjectives are for the press secretary afterward.
2. **Numbers are emotional objects here.** "3 RPM", "Batch 042", "<38°C"
   are *felt* as honesty (specificity → credibility), not read as data.
3. **Relief beats excitement.** For a staple, the winning feeling is the end
   of vigilance — design the calm of certainty, not the thrill of novelty.
4. **The guardian is the hero of every frame**, never the product. Oil is
   the instrument; the buyer's love and self-respect are the protagonists.
5. **Price certifies; it must never apologize** (E7) — no clutter of fake
   strikethroughs around the honest tiers.
